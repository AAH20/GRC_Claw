### 1.3 Key Observations from Market

1. **Awareness is crowded, middle is thin**: AIGP and CEET compete at the awareness/generalist level. GAICC owns the ISO 42001-specific niche. IEEE CertifAIEd owns the ethics assessment niche.
2. **Enterprise programs converge on tiered models**: AstraZeneca's Bronze→Diamond and J&J's mandatory-then-bootcamp pattern show the market standard: baseline for all, depth for specialists.
3. **Role-based is the differentiator**: Programs that scale (J&J, AZ, Novartis) segment by role — not one-size-fits-all.
4. **Audit evidence is the gap**: Most certifications prove knowledge, not competence. ISO 42001 auditors want per-person, per-role evidence with effectiveness evaluation.
5. **Modalities are multi-channel**: No single modality suffices. Enterprise programs blend self-paced, live, bootcamps, and AI-driven simulations.

## 2. ISO/IEC 42001:2023 Clause 7.2 & 7.3 — Requirements Analysis

### 2.1 Clause 7.2 — Competence

**Four duties:**
1. **Determine** the necessary competence of persons doing work under the organization's control that affects AI system performance
2. **Ensure** they are competent on the basis of appropriate education, training, or experience
3. **Act** where competence gaps exist (train, mentor, reassign, hire/contract) and **evaluate** the effectiveness of those actions
4. **Retain documented information** as evidence of competence

**Scope trigger**: "work that affects AI performance" — reaches beyond data science to anyone whose decisions shape model behavior in production.

**Key distinction**: Competence = demonstrated ability to do the job. Not attendance. Not a certificate alone. Evidence can be training, qualification, experience, or assessed work.

### 2.2 Clause 7.3 — Awareness

**Three things everyone must know:**
1. The **AI policy** (and relevant objectives)
2. Their own **contribution** to AIMS effectiveness (including benefits of improved AI performance)
3. The **implications of not conforming** to AIMS requirements

**Scope**: Everyone under the organization's control — broader population than 7.2, lighter content.

**Key distinction**: Awareness ≠ training. Awareness is about knowing why the rules exist and what your part is. A contractor may need awareness without needing competence in your processes.

### 2.3 Common Audit Findings

| Finding | Clause | Root Cause |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness stays within AI team | 7.3 | Training targeted only at technical staff |
| Attendance tracked without competence verification | 7.2 | No testing, no effectiveness evaluation |
| Competence assumed from job title | 7.2 | No competence matrix, no evidence |
| 7.2 and 7.3 evidence conflated | Both | Same record used for both requirements |
| Contractors excluded | Both | No pre-qualification or onboarding |
| No refresh on policy/system change | 7.3 | Static annual training, no trigger-based updates |

### 2.4 Evidence Auditors Expect

**For 7.2 (Competence):**
- Competence matrix linking roles to required competencies at defined levels
- Training records with certificates
- Qualifications and experience documentation
- Effectiveness evaluations (test results, assessments, performance reviews)
- Records of corrective actions taken and their outcomes

**For 7.3 (Awareness):**
- Awareness programme plan and schedule
- Completion records and acknowledgments
- Evidence of policy communication (emails, briefings, campaign materials)
- Records of periodic reminders and updates
- Evidence that awareness reached beyond technical teams

## 3. GRC_Claw Role-Based Training Framework

### 3.1 Design Principles

1. **Role-first, not course-first**: Curriculum is derived from role competence requirements, not from available courses
2. **Two-track separation**: Competence (7.2) and Awareness (7.3) are tracked separately with different evidence
3. **Evidence-by-design**: Every module produces audit-ready artifacts — not just completion records
4. **Tiered depth**: Three tiers — Awareness (all), Working (practitioners), Expert (governance/audit)
5. **Continuous refresh**: Trigger-based updates (new system, policy change, incident) + scheduled refresh
6. **Multi-modal**: Self-paced, live, hands-on labs, and AI-driven scenarios blended by role need

### 3.2 Role Taxonomy

| Role Tier | Roles | 7.2 Competence | 7.3 Awareness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 0: All Staff** | Every employee, contractor, temporary worker | Baseline AI literacy | Full 7.3 coverage |
| **Tier 1: AI Users** | Business users, analysts, operations staff, customer-facing roles | Working-level: responsible use, output validation, escalation | Full 7.3 + role-specific policy |
| **Tier 2: AI Practitioners** | Data scientists, ML engineers, MLOps, data engineers, AI product managers | Expert-level: model governance, data provenance, testing, monitoring | Full 7.3 + technical policy depth |
| **Tier 3: AI Governance** | AI governance committee, AIMS manager, risk owners, ethics/legal reviewers, internal auditors | Expert-level: AIMS implementation, audit, risk assessment, compliance | Full 7.3 + governance-specific |
| **Tier 4: Leadership** | C-suite, board, senior executives | Strategic: AI strategy, risk appetite, capital allocation | Full 7.3 + strategic context |

### 3.6 Evidence Architecture (Audit-Ready)

#### Evidence Chain per Person

```
Role Assignment → Competence Requirements (from matrix) → 
Learning Path Assigned → Modules Completed → Assessments Passed → 
Competence Verified → Evidence Stored → Refresh Scheduled → 
Effectiveness Evaluated → Management Review Input
```

#### Evidence Types by Clause

**7.2 Competence Evidence:**
1. **Competence matrix** — role-to-competency mapping with required levels
2. **Training records** — per person, per module: completion date, score, certificate
3. **Qualifications** — external certifications (GAICC, IAPP, IEEE, CEET)
4. **Experience documentation** — CV, project assignments, role history
5. **Assessed work** — artifacts produced (risk assessments, audit reports, bias evaluations)
6. **Effectiveness evaluations** — test scores, supervisor assessments, performance reviews
7. **Corrective action records** — gaps identified, actions taken, re-evaluation results

**7.3 Awareness Evidence:**
1. **Awareness programme plan** — schedule, audience, content, delivery method
2. **Completion records** — per person, per awareness activity
3. **Policy acknowledgments** — signed/dated acknowledgments of AI policy
4. **Communication records** — emails, briefings, campaign materials
5. **Manager attestations** — confirmation that reinforcement conversations occurred
6. **Trigger-based update records** — evidence that policy changes reached affected staff

#### Evidence Storage Requirements

| Requirement | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Controlled document | Versioned, approved, accessible (Clause 7.5) |
| Retention | Minimum 3 years or per regulatory requirement |
| Access | Role-based: individual sees own records; managers see team; auditors see all |
| Integrity | Tamper-evident logs; no retroactive edits |
| Availability | Exportable for audit; per-cohort dossier format |

### 3.7 Effectiveness Evaluation (7.2 Requirement)

| Evaluation Method | Frequency | Audience | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knowledge test (quiz) | Per module | All | Score record |
| Scenario assessment | Per role-specific module | Tiers 1–3 | Assessment score + assessor notes |
| Practical artifact review | Per project | Tiers 2–3 | Artifact quality rating |
| Supervisor observation | Semi-annual | Tiers 1–3 | Observation record |
| 360-degree feedback | Annual | Tiers 2–3 | Feedback summary |
| Incident/audit finding review | Per event | Relevant roles | Finding + corrective action record |
| Training satisfaction survey | Per module | All | Survey results (supplementary) |

### 3.8 Continuous Refresh Triggers

| Trigger | Action | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| New AI system deployed | Targeted awareness for affected roles | Before go-live |
| AI policy updated | Policy update briefing for all | Within 30 days |
| AI incident occurred | Lessons learned briefing | Within 14 days |
| New regulation published | Impact assessment + targeted training | Within 60 days |
| Role change | New role competence assessment | Within 30 days of change |
| Annual cycle | Scheduled refresh training | Per annual plan |
| Audit finding | Corrective training | Per corrective action plan |
| Technology shift (e.g., agentic AI) | Curriculum update + pilot training | Per technology watch |

## 5. Key Differentiators of This Framework

1. **ISO 42001-native**: Designed from Clause 7.2/7.3 requirements, not retrofitted
2. **Role-based competence matrix**: 10 competencies × 5 tiers with defined levels — auditors can trace any person to any requirement
3. **Dual-track evidence**: Competence and awareness evidence stored separately, satisfying the common audit finding of conflation
4. **External certification integration**: GAICC Lead Implementer/Internal Auditor certifications provide third-party validation of Tier 3 competence
5. **Effectiveness evaluation built-in**: Not just completion tracking — testing, artifact review, and supervisor observation
6. **Trigger-based refresh**: Policy changes, incidents, and new systems automatically trigger targeted awareness updates
7. **Audit-ready by design**: Every module produces specific evidence artifacts; per-cohort dossiers available for auditor review
8. **Scalable tiering**: Tier 0 is lightweight (2 hours); depth is added only where role requires it

## 7. Recommendations

1. **Adopt GAICC as primary certification partner** for Tier 3 — it is the only certification directly aligned to ISO 42001, providing the strongest audit evidence
2. **Use IAPP AIGP as supplementary** for Tier 2 practitioners who need broader governance knowledge beyond ISO 42001
3. **Consider IEEE CertifAIEd** if GRC_Claw needs product-level ethics certification for AI systems it deploys or procures
4. **Use CertNexus CEET** as optional Tier 1 validation — vendor-neutral, low cost, but limited audit evidence value
5. **Prioritize the competence matrix** — this is the single most important artifact for audit readiness
6. **Invest in evidence infrastructure** before content — an LMS that cannot produce per-person, per-role evidence with effectiveness evaluation will fail audit
7. **Start with Tier 3** — certify the governance team first (GAICC Lead Implementer), as they will design and deliver the rest of the program
8. **Embed awareness in onboarding** — new hires should complete M01/M02 within their first week, not at annual refresh
